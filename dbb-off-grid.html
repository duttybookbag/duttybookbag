Design Ratios & Principles

J-Tube: 1:2:4 (Feed:Burn:Riser) — most proven design

L-Tube: 1:1.5:3 — better fuel control, less smoke-back

K-Style: 1.2:2:3.5 — self-feeding angled design

Cooking: 1:1.5:2.5 — optimized for cooking height

Height Rule: Chamber 7-8x taller than wide

Air Intake: 25-30% of chamber cross-section

Heat Riser: Must match chamber diameter

Insulation: Critical for secondary combustion

J-Tube Design

Standard rocket stove with horizontal feed tube, vertical burn tunnel, and heat riser. Best for general purpose heating and cooking. Requires proper draft for secondary combustion.

L-Tube Design

Simplified design with direct feed into burn tunnel. Better fuel control and less smoke-back risk. Ideal for cooking where heat control is important.

K-Style Design

Features angled feed chute for automatic stick feeding. Self-feeding but can jam with irregular sticks. Good for continuous burning.

Material Tips

Steel: 1/4" thick minimum for combustion chamber. Fire brick: 2-4" walls. Insulation is critical — use perlite, vermiculite, or ceramic fiber between walls.

Air Intake Guidelines

Primary air: 25-33% of cross-sectional area. Secondary air: 5-7%. Too much air cools combustion. Too little creates smoke.

Safety & Performance

Proper ratios ensure clean combustion with minimal smoke. Metal systems need insulation and eventually wear out. Test outdoors first.

⚠ SAFETY: Rocket stoves generate extreme heat (1400-1800°F). Build with proper materials. Keep away from combustibles. Test outdoors. Never use indoors without proper ventilation and chimney. DuttyBookBag assumes no liability.

Required Parts & Materials

1. Inner Heat Exchanger Tube
6" diameter x 10" long steel pipe. Schedule 40 or thicker recommended.
2. Aluminum Drip Feed Tube
1/4" to 3/8" aluminum tubing. Length: funnel to back of burn tunnel.
3. Ball Valve
1/4" brass ball valve for precise drip rate control.
4. Metal Funnel
4" top opening, 2.5" outlet. Fabricated from 16ga steel sheet.
5. Oil Reservoir
5-gallon steel tank with gravity feed. Must be above stove.
6. Air Blower
12V DC blower, 1-2 CFM. Computer fan or marine bilge blower.
7. Air Control Damper
Adjustable butterfly valve for combustion air regulation.
8. Insulation Material
Perlite, vermiculite, or ceramic fiber for 2" heat exchanger gap.
9. Power Supply
12V DC adapter or battery for blower motor.
10. Burn Pan
Small steel pan or cast iron dish to catch and burn dripped oil.
11. Mounting Hardware
Brackets, bolts, and welding supplies for securing components.
12. Temperature Gauge
High-temp thermometer (optional) for monitoring burn chamber.

Assembly Sequence

  1. Prepare Heat Exchanger: Insert 6" steel pipe inside your existing 10" cylinder. Center it and weld support tabs. Fill 2" gap with insulation (perlite or vermiculite). Seal top and bottom with steel plates, leaving air inlet at bottom and outlet at top.
  2. Install Oil Feed System: Fabricate removable funnel to fit over existing feed opening. Run aluminum tube from funnel through burn tunnel to back wall. Install ball valve in line for drip control. Mount oil reservoir above stove level.
  3. Mount Air Blower: Install 12V blower at existing bottom square opening. Add air control damper in line. Connect 12V power supply with variable speed control. Test air flow before proceeding.
  4. Install Burn Pan: Place small steel pan at end of aluminum drip tube inside burn tunnel. This catches and burns the dripped oil. Pan should be removable for cleaning.
  5. Connect Hot Air Outlet: Install hot air outlet pipe at top of heat exchanger. This can connect to ducting for distributing heated air. Add temperature gauge if desired.
  6. Initial Testing: Fill oil reservoir with clean waste oil. Start with paper and diesel in burn pan to preheat chamber. Once hot (400°F+), start oil drip at 1-2 drops per second. Adjust air flow for clean blue flame.
  7. Fine Tuning: Adjust drip rate and air flow for optimal burn. Clean blue flame with no smoke indicates proper operation. Oil should vaporize completely with no pooling in pan.
  8. Safety Setup: Install fire extinguisher nearby. Ensure adequate ventilation. Test emergency shutdown procedures. Never leave unattended during initial break-in period.

Performance Data

Heat Output: 30,000-50,000 BTU/hr

Oil Consumption: 0.3-0.5 gallons/hour

Burn Temp: 1400-1800°F

Hot Air Output: 150-200°F

Air Flow Required: 1-2 CFM

Startup Time: 5-10 minutes

Fuel Types: Motor oil, hydraulic oil, vegetable oil

Safety Requirements

⚠ Clearances: 36" from combustibles

⚠ Ventilation: Adequate fresh air supply

⚠ Fire Safety: Class B extinguisher required

⚠ Fuel Storage: Sealed containers only

⚠ Never Leave: Unattended when operating

⚠ Pre-Heat: Always pre-heat before oil feed

⚠ Shutdown: Stop oil first, then air
